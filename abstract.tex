Silicon photonics provides a scalable and cost-efficient platform for high-bandwidth, low-power data transmission, making it a promising solution for next-generation data center interconnects.
In particular, circuit switching enables direct optical connectivity for both scale-up and scale-out network architectures, avoiding optical–electrical–optical conversions.
However, the practical deployment of large-scale photonic switching fabrics is limited not only by hardware capabilities, but also by the lack of scalable programming and control methodologies.

This thesis addresses these limitations by introducing a graph-based programming methodology that translates photonic circuits into weighted graphs, providing a rigorous mathematical abstraction of complex photonic circuit fabrics.
By representing photonic building blocks and their connections as graph elements with associated physical metrics,
this approach enables high-level programming and systematic optimization of device architectures and control methodologies, facilitating efficient and scalable design and analysis of photonic circuits.

Based on this methodology, a set of routing and configuration algorithms is developed to support optical interconnects, multicasting, and circuit switching within general-purpose photonic processors, demonstrating that diverse networking functions can be realized on a single hardware platform without manual intervention.

While this flexibility of general-purpose architectures enables multi-functional operation, it also introduces inherent challenges in scaling to large, high-radix switching fabrics
To move beyond limited-scale programmability, the work further establishes a unified approach for modeling and benchmarking high-radix photonic switch architectures.
By combining graph representations with physical-layer metrics such as insertion loss, crosstalk, and signal-to-noise ratio, it provides a systematic way to evaluate scalability trade-offs across different topologies and routing strategies,
thereby offering practical design guidance for switching fabrics that balance hardware complexity and optical performance under realistic network conditions.

Finally, we extend these concepts to system integration by implementing a software-defined control plane based on model-driven interfaces and gNMI.
This allows photonic switches to be managed as programmable network elements, aligning Layer-1 optical resources with existing SDN workflows, supporting dynamic interconnect reconfiguration and monitoring.

Our work establishes a scalable and manageable foundation for integrating photonic circuit switching into next-generation AI networking systems.
